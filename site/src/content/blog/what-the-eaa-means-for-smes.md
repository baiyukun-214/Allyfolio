The **European Accessibility Act (EAA)** has applied since **28 June 2025**. In Germany it is implemented by the **Barrierefreiheitsstärkungsgesetz (BFSG)**.

## Who is affected?

The EAA covers many consumer-facing products and services, including online shops, banking services, e-books and passenger transport services.

**Microenterprises that provide services are exempt** from the service requirements. A microenterprise has fewer than 10 staff *and* an annual turnover or balance sheet total of no more than €2 million. If that is you, accessibility is still good for your customers – but check with an adviser before spending money.

## What does "accessible" mean in practice?

Most organisations use the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) as the technical benchmark, via the European standard EN 301 549.

## What automated testing can do

Tools like axe-core can find many common problems quickly: missing form labels, low colour contrast, missing alt attributes. According to a [Deque study](https://www.deque.com/automated-accessibility-testing-coverage/), automated testing finds roughly **57% of issues**.

The other part needs people: is the alt text meaningful? Is the focus order logical? Can a screen-reader user complete checkout?

## Avoid overlays

Overlay widgets promise a fix with one line of code. They do not change your underlying markup and many disabled users find them get in the way. Fix the source instead.

## A practical plan

1. Scan your key pages and journeys.
2. Fix the issues found, starting with the most severe.
3. Do a manual review of the same journeys.
4. Publish an accessibility statement and keep a record of your work.
5. Re-test regularly.
